\begin{abstract}
Source apportionment from sparse urban air-quality sensors is an inverse problem whose resolution is limited by sensor placement, wind-driven transport, background variation, and noise.  Known or proxy emission inventories restrict the unknown source field to a finite set of candidate groups, making attribution meaningful, but do not guarantee that those groups are distinguishable from the available observations.  We represent time-varying source activity with a low-dimensional nonnegative temporal basis and formulate inventory-based source apportionment as a wind-conditioned lagged inverse problem in which each source--basis coefficient produces a sensor-time fingerprint.  After projecting out a separate low-dimensional background space, the governing object is the projected lagged source--basis response matrix (\(\widetilde H_\Phi\)): exact identifiability at the chosen temporal-basis resolution requires its full column rank, while noise-robust attribution is controlled by its singular values, coefficient visibility, background absorption, and pairwise fingerprint coherence.  Building on this analysis, we propose an identifiability-aware apportionment (IASA) framework that estimates nonnegative source--basis coefficients, reconstructs source activity trajectories, and reports uncertainty with conservative deterministic grouping recommendations for indistinguishable sources.  We instantiate it on a New Delhi platform (government PM\(_{2.5}\) and wind observations, regulatory sensor locations, and four proxy source groups with declared temporal activity bases) with controlled and observed-data evaluations of recovery, ambiguity, wind diversity, background stress, transport error, inventory robustness, and residual model adequacy.  The framework reports the attribution resolution defensible under the declared inventories, transport, background, lag, and noise model rather than the most detailed possible attribution vector.
\end{abstract}
