Previous submission: AAAI 2027, rejected in Phase 1 (ratings 4 and 5, plus an AI review). The paper has been rewritten for the applications track. Main criticisms and changes:

1. Novelty of the theory. Both reviewers found the results elementary (rank and conditioning facts applied to one setting). The new theory concerns groupings of sources: Theorem 5.7 shows that a grouping is identifiable exactly when the column spaces of its blocks form a direct sum, and that the finest identifiable grouping is unique, equals the connected components of the column matroid, and is computable in O(NJ^2); for sums of coefficients, minimal groupings can be non-unique and deciding whether one with at least two blocks exists is NP-complete (Appendix A). Theorem 5.9 bounds a group's signal error by the noise divided by the group's separation (the sine of a principal angle), with a uniform high-probability version that also covers correlated noise, and Proposition 5.12 gives a condition under which the reportable groupings are unchanged by an operator error. Related work now positions the method against Henry's eligible space, averaging kernels and aggregation-error methods.

2. The grouping heuristic was not identifiable (the AI review's counterexample for summed activities). The reported quantity is now each group's signal (in micrograms per cubic metre, unaffected by inventory rescaling), which Theorem 5.7 shows is identifiable. Groups are chosen from the matroid components and a separation threshold justified by Theorem 5.9.

3. Background-stress result (near-zero share error while a source direction was absorbed). The absorbed coefficient is now flagged as not identified, and the paper reports only group-signal errors (0.4-6.0%), which Theorem 5.9(a) shows do not depend on the choice of solution.

4. Baseline comparison not symmetric. All baseline formulations are defined in the main text, and each method is scored on the blocks it reports, IASA on its partition and the baselines on the individual sources. Under background stress, plain NNLS on the projected system reports the same two sources as IASA, with group-signal errors within 0.003 of IASA's, but raises no flag. For the nearly proportional pair under steady wind, IASA merges the two sources and the merged signal has error 0.44%, whereas plain NNLS reports them separately with errors of 3.8% and 6.9%. The mass balance has errors of 36-784%, which the paper attributes to the absence of background removal.

5. No ground truth for New Delhi. The observed weeks are presented as a demonstration with explicit statistical statements: Theorem 5.9(c) bounds against the fitted signals (the bound establishes the largest group's signal in two of four weeks), bootstrap intervals for the shares, a sensitivity level for temporally correlated noise, and the residual reported as 1.5-3.0 times the noise level. No claim of physical accuracy is made.

6. Dependence on the declared model and unmodelled sources. The assumptions are now stated formally (Assumption 5.1: declared model, baseline offset, observed rows independent of the noise; Assumption 5.2: Gaussian noise with the declared level at least the true one). Experiment 8 shows that the adequacy test rejects omitted sources outside the model span (rate 1.00) and cannot detect sources inside it (rate 0.05), and the paper states this limitation. Proposition 5.12 and Experiment 5 address wind and dispersion error; a sensitivity analysis of the observed results to wind-imputation error is listed as not done.

7. Smaller points. The estimator is unregularized NNLS in all runs, which the bounds cover; thresholds and the share error are defined in the main text; the lag is stated as declared (12 hours), with the convergence rule reported as a diagnostic; and the observed results now carry uncertainty intervals.
